01 · The Playbook

The four levers attackers pull

Phishing rarely relies on technical exploits — it relies on psychology. Almost every attack leans on one or more of these social-engineering levers.

Lever 01

Urgency

"Act within 24 hours or your account will be suspended." Panic shortens the time you spend thinking.

Lever 02

Authority

Impersonating a CEO, IT admin, or bank to borrow their credibility and discourage questioning.

Lever 03

Scarcity / Reward

Fake prizes, refunds, or limited-time offers that trigger impulsive clicks before scrutiny.

Lever 04

Trust Transfer

Spoofing a known brand's logo, tone, and layout so the message inherits trust it didn't earn.

02 · The Evidence

Spot the phish — inspect each exhibit

Below are four real-world-style email samples. Read each one first, then tap "Reveal evidence" to see exactly which details gave it away — highlighted the way an investigator would mark a case file.

Exhibit A — Account SuspensionPHISHING
Fromsecurity@paypa1-alerts.com
Toyou@yourcompany.com
SubjectUrgent: Your account will be suspended in 24 hours
Dear Customer,

We detected unusual activity on your account. You must verify your information within 24 hours or your account will be permanently suspended.

Click here to verify: http://paypal-secure-login.verify-account.info

Failure to act will result in loss of access to your funds.

— PayPal Security Teem
  • Lookalike domain — "paypa1-alerts.com" uses a digit "1" instead of "l", and isn't PayPal's real domain.
  • Manufactured urgency — a tight, scary deadline is designed to stop you from thinking it through.
  • Suspicious link — the visible text says "PayPal" but the actual domain (verify-account.info) has nothing to do with PayPal.
  • Spelling/grammar slip — "Security Teem" — legitimate companies proofread their official emails.
Exhibit B — CEO Wire RequestPHISHING (Spear / BEC)
FromJ. Whitfield <j.whitfield@corp-outlook.net>
Toyou@yourcompany.com
SubjectQuick favor
Hi,

I'm in back-to-back board meetings and can't talk right now. I need you to process an urgent wire transfer for a vendor before end of day — don't mention this to anyone else on the finance team yet, I'll explain later.

Reply with your direct cell so I can send the details.

Thanks,
James (sent from my iPhone)
  • Authority impersonation + lookalike domain — claims to be an executive but uses a personal/external domain, not the real company domain.
  • Unverifiable unavailability — "can't talk right now" pre-empts the one thing that would expose the scam: a phone call to confirm.
  • Isolation tactic — asking you to bypass normal approval channels and stay quiet is a major red flag for Business Email Compromise (BEC).
Exhibit C — Prize NotificationPHISHING
Fromrewards@amazon-giftcenter.com
Toyou@yourcompany.com
Subject🎉 You've been selected for a $500 gift card!
Congratulations!

Your email was randomly selected to receive a $500 Amazon gift card — claim within 3 hours before this offer expires.

To claim, simply confirm your identity by entering your full name, date of birth, and card details on our secure claim page.

www.amazon-giftcenter.com/claim
  • Unofficial domain — Amazon never sends prize claims from third-party domains like "amazon-giftcenter.com".
  • Reward + scarcity combo — "randomly selected" plus a ticking clock is a classic combination to override skepticism.
  • Data harvesting request — no legitimate giveaway needs your card details or date of birth to "verify identity".
Exhibit D — IT Password ResetLEGITIMATE (control sample)
Fromit-helpdesk@yourcompany.com
Toyou@yourcompany.com
SubjectScheduled password rotation — action needed by Friday
Hi,

As part of our quarterly security policy, your password is due to expire on Friday. Please update it any time before then via the company SSO portal (the same login page you use every day — no link provided here).

If you have questions, open a ticket through the IT Helpdesk portal or call ext. 204.

— IT Helpdesk
  • Internal domain match — sender matches the real company domain exactly.
  • No embedded link or attachment — directs you to the known portal you already use, instead of a new "verification" link.
  • Reasonable timeframe + an alternate verification path (ticket/phone extension) — no artificial urgency, and a way to confirm independently.
03 · Field Manual

Five habits that stop most phishing cold

You don't need to be a security expert — you need a short list of reflexes.

01

Check the actual domainHover over links and sender addresses — read the part right before ".com", not just the display name.

02

Slow down on urgencyDeadlines under 24 hours, threats of suspension, or "don't tell anyone" requests are a deliberate pressure tactic.

03

Verify out-of-bandFor money, credentials, or policy-bending requests, confirm via a separate channel — call the person, don't reply to the email.

04

Never enter credentials from a linkNavigate to the site yourself by typing the known URL instead of clicking through an email.

05

Report, don't just deleteForward suspicious emails to your security team — one report can protect the rest of the company.
